# Empirical Results

## Baseline 12–2 Momentum

The baseline analysis evaluates whether conventional 12–2 momentum predicts one-month-ahead returns within a dynamic universe of the 1,000 largest eligible U.S. common stocks by lagged market capitalization. The reported sample runs from January 2001 through December 2025, with momentum measured using cumulative returns from $t-12$ through $t-2$.

Signal availability was high throughout the sample. On average, 98.1% of universe observations had a valid 12–2 momentum signal, and approximately 98.1% had both a valid signal and observed outcome return. Missing signals were concentrated among securities with insufficient return histories, particularly recent listings, rather than arising from panel-construction problems.

The baseline signal produced a small positive cross-sectional relationship with subsequent returns. The mean monthly Spearman Rank IC was **0.0122**, with a t-statistic of **1.12**, while **54.7%** of months had positive ICs. These results indicate that higher-momentum stocks tended to rank somewhat better on next-month returns, although the full-sample relationship was weak statistically.

Equal-weighted portfolio sorts showed a similar pattern. Average monthly returns ranged from **0.62%** in the lowest-momentum quintile to **0.95%** in the highest-momentum quintile. The resulting Q5-minus-Q1 portfolio earned an average **0.338% per month**, corresponding to an annualized arithmetic mean spread of approximately **4.06%**. Its monthly-return t-statistic was **1.05**, and the long-short portfolio was positive in **56.7%** of months.

The quintile pattern was not perfectly monotonic across all five portfolios. Instead, the clearest separation appeared between the lowest-momentum group and the higher-momentum portfolios. This is consistent with the relatively small average Rank IC: momentum provides some cross-sectional information, but does not generate a uniformly strong ranking across the entire universe.

## Time Variation

Momentum performance varied substantially over time.

The 12-month rolling mean Rank IC moved repeatedly between positive and negative regimes. Predictive strength was particularly strong during portions of the early and mid-2000s, while pronounced reversals occurred around the financial-crisis period and again in several later episodes.

![12-month rolling mean Rank IC](../results/figures/rolling_rank_ic_12m.png)

The same pattern appears in the Q5-minus-Q1 portfolio. The rolling 12-month mean long-short return reached strongly positive levels during favorable momentum regimes but became sharply negative during reversal periods. The weakest rolling window occurred around 2009, when the 12-month average Q5-minus-Q1 return fell to roughly **−5.3%** per month.

![12-month rolling Q5-minus-Q1 return](../results/figures/rolling_long_short_12m.png)

Monthly Rank IC and Q5-minus-Q1 returns were highly correlated, with a correlation of approximately **0.93**. This is expected because both measure different manifestations of the same underlying cross-sectional relationship, but it also provides a useful consistency check between the ranking and portfolio analyses.

Subperiod results reinforce the evidence of time variation. Mean Rank IC was **0.0280** during 2001–2008, compared with only **0.0046** during 2009–2016 and **0.0049** during 2017–2025. The baseline signal therefore appears substantially stronger in the earlier part of the sample than in later periods.

## Equal-Weighted and Value-Weighted Portfolios

To test whether the baseline portfolio result was driven primarily by relatively smaller stocks within the Top-1000 universe, the quintile portfolios were also constructed using lagged market capitalization weights.

The average value-weighted Q5-minus-Q1 return was **0.298% per month**, compared with **0.338%** under equal weighting. Its t-statistic was **0.90**, versus **1.05** for the equal-weighted strategy. Monthly equal- and value-weighted long-short returns had a correlation of approximately **0.93**.

The similarity of the two specifications suggests that the positive momentum spread is not solely generated by the smaller stocks within the baseline universe, although value weighting slightly weakens the magnitude of the effect.

![Average momentum quintile returns](../results/figures/average_quintile_returns.png)

Both weighting schemes show a broadly similar cross-sectional structure: the lowest-momentum portfolio has the weakest average return, while the highest-momentum portfolio has the strongest. The middle quintiles are not strictly monotonic, again consistent with the modest full-sample Rank IC.

## Formation-Window Robustness

The baseline 12–2 specification was compared with a shorter 6–2 momentum signal using returns from $t-6$ through $t-2$.

The 6–2 specification retained the same direction of the effect but produced weaker results. Mean Rank IC declined from **0.0122** to **0.0058**, while the equal-weighted Q5-minus-Q1 spread fell from **0.338%** to **0.249%** per month. The corresponding long-short t-statistic declined from **1.05** to **0.85**.

Signal coverage increased from **98.1%** under 12–2 momentum to approximately **99.1%** under 6–2 momentum because the shorter formation period requires less historical return data.

Overall, shortening the formation window does not eliminate the positive relationship, but the conventional 12–2 specification produces the stronger signal in this sample.

## Universe-Size Robustness

The baseline Top-1000 universe was also compared with Top-500 and Top-1500 specifications while holding the 12–2 signal definition and portfolio methodology fixed.

The results were stable across all three universes:

| Universe | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Mean Q5−Q1 / month | Q5−Q1 t-stat |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Top 500 | 0.0146 | 1.23 | 0.391% | 1.24 |
| Top 1000 | 0.0122 | 1.12 | 0.338% | 1.05 |
| Top 1500 | 0.0134 | 1.32 | 0.346% | 1.06 |

The long-short spread remained between approximately **0.34% and 0.39% per month**, while mean Rank IC remained between **0.012 and 0.015**. The results therefore do not appear to depend materially on selecting exactly 1,000 securities.

Signal coverage declined modestly as the universe broadened – from **98.8%** in the Top-500 universe to **97.4%** in the Top-1500 universe – which is consistent with broader universes containing more securities with shorter available return histories.

## Interpretation

Overall, conventional cross-sectional momentum is present in the sample, but the evidence is modest rather than overwhelming. Higher-momentum stocks tend to outperform lower-momentum stocks on average, and the result survives reasonable changes in portfolio weighting, formation horizon, and universe size.

At the same time, the full-sample Rank IC and long-short t-statistics are well below conventional significance thresholds. Momentum performance is also strongly time-varying, with substantial reversal episodes and noticeably weaker average predictive power after 2008.

The evidence therefore supports a nuanced conclusion: 12–2 momentum contains economically meaningful cross-sectional information in this universe, but its predictive strength is unstable through time and relatively weak when evaluated over the full 2001–2025 sample.
